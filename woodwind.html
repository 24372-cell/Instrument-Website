Woodwind

Explore the instruments in the woodwind family

Flute/Piccolo

The Flute is a graceful woodwind instrument that produces bright, airy sounds when a player blows a stream of air across its lip plate. As one of the oldest known musical instruments in human history, it evolved from simple carved bone and wood tubes into sophisticated metal models, as well as distinct family variations like the piccolo, a miniature flute that plays a full octave higher with a brilliant, piercing tone. By pressing the interlocking keys to open and close tone holes, musicians change the length of the vibrating air column to create a wide and expressive range of notes across both standard and specialized versions of the instrument.

Oboe

The oboe is a slender, black woodwind instrument that looks very similar to a clarinet, but it creates a completely different, uniquely enchanting sound.Instead of a standard mouthpiece, it uses a double reed—two tiny pieces of cane bound together—which the player blows through to produce a distinct, singing tone that can sound beautifully haunting or brightly playful. It is a vital member of the symphony orchestra, famous for playing expressive solos and traditionally sounding the very first note to help all the other instruments tune up.

Clarinet

The clarinet is a sleek woodwind instrument, usually made of black wood or plastic, featuring a flared bell at the bottom and a series of shiny metal keys.Unlike the oboe, it uses a single reed attached to a flat mouthpiece to create its smooth, warm, and velvety sound. It is one of the most versatile instruments in the world, comfortably shifting from the rich, deep notes of a classical symphony orchestra to the bright, energetic riffs of a jazz band. Because its keys are easy to press and it is highly adaptable, the clarinet is incredibly popular for beginners.

Bassoon

The bassoon is the large, low-pitched grandfather of the woodwind family, easily recognized by its long, heavy wooden body and a curved metal pipe that holds its double reed.Because it is roughly 1.3 metres tall (and contains nearly 2.5 metres of tubing folded in half), it produces a remarkably deep, rich, and resonant sound that anchors the bass section of a symphony orchestra. It is a highly expressive instrument, capable of shifting from a somber, haunting melody to a bouncy, comical rhythm in a single breath.

Saxophone

The saxophone is a striking member of the woodwind family that bridges the gap between brass and wood instruments, featuring a gleaming brass body but using a single reed and mouthpiece just like a clarinet.Invented in the 1840s, it is a relatively modern instrument designed to combine the powerful acoustic projection of brass with the agile fingerwork of woodwinds. It produces a incredibly smooth, warm, and expressive tone that can transition instantly from a smoky, soulful whisper to a roaring, energetic shout. While it occasionally appears in classical symphonies, the saxophone is the absolute superstar of jazz, blues, and rock bands.
