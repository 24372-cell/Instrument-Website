Violin

The violin is a four-stringed, bowed acoustic instrument that plays the highest notes in the string family. To understand it fully, you must know its physical construction, essential gear, and maintenance needs. A standard violin consists of a hollow wooden body—traditionally built with a spruce top for resonance and maple sides and back—along with a neck, fingerboard, bridge, and pegbox. Sound is produced when you draw a bow across the strings or pluck them, sending vibrations through the bridge into the body to amplify the tone. You cannot play the violin alone; you also need a functional bow (made of wood or carbon fiber), rosin (a sticky sap applied to the bow hair to create friction against the strings), a shoulder rest for comfortable posture, and a digital tuner or pitch pipe.
